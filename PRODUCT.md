# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hungry locals and visitors looking for a place to eat. They browse on phones while deciding where to go, or on desktops when planning ahead. Primary job: find appealing food, check hours/location, and make a reservation or call. Secondary job: get a feel for the atmosphere and menu before visiting.

## Product Purpose

Drive foot traffic and reservations by making the restaurant look appetizing, approachable, and easy to reach. Success = a visitor books a table, calls, or remembers the name.

## Brand Personality

Casual, warm, unpretentious. Three words: approachable, flavorful, honest. The site should feel like walking into a neighborhood spot where the staff knows your name — not a white-tablecloth temple of cuisine.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS-template restaurant sites (identical card grids, big hero metrics, stock food photography feel)
- Overly fine-dining stiffness (too much whitespace, tiny type, pretentious copy)
- AI-generated slop (gradient text, glassmorphism for decoration, dark glows everywhere, identical icon-heading-text cards)
- Template-marketplace themes that scream "I bought this for $29"

## Design Principles

1. **Show the food first** — imagery and appetite appeal beat clever layout every time.
2. **Casual confidence** — warm tones and friendly typography, not sterile minimalism or overwrought decoration.
3. **Friction-free to act** — reservation, call, and directions should always be one tap away.
4. **Personality over polish** — a slightly imperfect, human feel beats a perfectly generic template.
5. **Mobile-first hunger** — most visitors are deciding on-the-go; load fast, read fast, act fast.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA target. Ensure sufficient color contrast on dark backgrounds, readable type sizes on mobile, and reduced-motion respect for vestibular sensitivities.
